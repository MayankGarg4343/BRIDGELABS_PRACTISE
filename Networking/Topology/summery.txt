STAR:
Central switch/device.
Data usually goes device → central device → destination.
Common in LANs.

RING:
Devices form a circle.
Data travels node-to-node around the ring.
Used in some specialized/resilient networks.

BUS:
All devices share one backbone.
Simple but poor scalability/reliability.
Traditional Ethernet; mostly obsolete today.

MESH:
Multiple interconnections.
Multiple paths.
High redundancy but expensive/complex.

TREE:
Hierarchical structure.
Core → Distribution → Access → End Devices.
Scalable and common as an architecture.

HYBRID:
Combination of multiple topology types.
Very common in real-world large networks.

KEY IDEA:
Topology tells us how devices are connected and what paths
traffic can take.

DEVOPS CONNECTION:

- Redundancy
- Single points of failure
