# MAN — Metropolitan Area Network

Ab PAN → LAN ke baad next logical network type hai **MAN**. Isko sirf “LAN se bada network” yaad karna enough nahi hai. Practical networking mein MAN ko samajhne ke liye **geographic scope, inter-building connectivity, fiber, Layer 2/Layer 3, service-provider networks, routing, redundancy, Metro Ethernet aur DevOps/data-center connectivity** ko connect karna important hai.

---

# 1. Simple Definition

**MAN = Metropolitan Area Network**

MAN ek network hai jo **ek city ya metropolitan area ke relatively large geographic region** mein multiple LANs ko connect karta hai.

Simple mental model:

```text
                 MAN
                  |
        +---------+---------+
        |         |         |
      LAN 1     LAN 2     LAN 3
        |         |         |
     Office A  Office B  Data Center
```

Matlab:

> **LAN ek building/organization ke andar local connectivity provide karta hai, while MAN multiple LANs/sites ko metropolitan/city-level area mein connect kar sakta hai.**

---

# 2. PAN → LAN → MAN → WAN

Network types ko geographic scope ke perspective se dekho:

```text
PAN
 |
 | Person ke around
 |
 ↓
LAN
 |
 | Home / Office / Building
 |
 ↓
MAN
 |
 | City / Metropolitan Area
 |
 ↓
WAN
 |
 | Country / Continent / Global
 |
 ↓
Internet
```

Roughly:

```text
PAN  → very small
LAN  → local
MAN  → metropolitan/city
WAN  → large geographic
```

Important:

> Ye strict physical hierarchy nahi hai. Ye primarily **network coverage/scope classification** hai.

# 8. MAN ka Typical Geographic Scope

MAN generally:

* City
* Metropolitan region
* Large urban area
* Multiple nearby campuses
* Multiple branches within a city

# 9. MAN mein Kaunsi Technologies Use Hoti Hain?

Different technologies MAN implement kar sakti hain.

Common examples include:

* Metro Ethernet
* Fiber-optic networks
* Carrier Ethernet
* MPLS-based provider networks
* SDH/SONET in traditional telecom environments
* DWDM-based optical transport
* Wireless metropolitan links
* IP/MPLS services

Technology depends on:

* Provider
* Distance
* Required bandwidth
* Redundancy
* SLA
* Cost
* Application
* Security requirements

# 10. Metro Ethernet

MAN ke context mein **Metro Ethernet** extremely important term hai.

Metro Ethernet basically Ethernet-based connectivity ko metropolitan area mein extend karta hai.

Concept:

```text
Office A
   |
Ethernet
   |
Provider Metro Network
   |
Ethernet
   |
Office B
```

Customer ko conceptually Ethernet service mil sakti hai even though provider network city-wide ho.

# 12. MAN vs WAN

Ye interview mein important distinction hai.

## MAN

```text
City
 |
 +-- Office A
 +-- Office B
 +-- Data Center
```

## WAN

```text
City A
   |
WAN
   |
City B
   |
WAN
   |
Country B
```

Comparison:

| Feature              | MAN                                      | WAN                                                     |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Full form            | Metropolitan Area Network                | Wide Area Network                                       |
| Scope                | City/metropolitan                        | Large geographic                                        |
| Connects             | Multiple local sites                     | Cities/countries/regions                                |
| Typical technologies | Metro Ethernet, fiber, provider networks | MPLS, Internet, SD-WAN, leased links, optical transport |
| Latency              | Usually lower within metro region        | Often higher                                            |
| Ownership            | Enterprise/provider                      | Enterprise/provider/multiple carriers                   |
| Example              | City-wide branch network                 | Mumbai ↔ Delhi ↔ London                                 |

---

# 13. MAN vs LAN

| Feature        | LAN                      | MAN                                           |
| -------------- | ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- |
| Scope          | Building/home/local site | City/metropolitan                             |
| Typical size   | Small                    | Larger                                        |
| Connects       | Devices                  | Multiple LANs/sites                           |
| Common devices | Switches, APs            | Routers, provider switches, optical equipment |
| Common medium  | Ethernet, Wi-Fi          | Fiber, Metro Ethernet, provider links         |
| Latency        | Very low                 | Usually higher than LAN                       |
| Ownership      | Usually organization     | Organization/provider                         |
| Example        | Office network           | Multiple offices across a city                |

# 16. MAN Ring Topology

Historically and in carrier/transport architectures, ring designs have been common because they can provide redundancy.

Example:

```text
             Site A
            /      \
           /        \
       Site B ------ Site D
          \          /
           \        /
             Site C
```

If one link fails:

```text
Site A ----X---- Site B
```

Traffic may potentially take another path:

```text
Site A
  |
  ↓
Site D
  |
  ↓
Site C
  |
  ↓
Site B
```

This is one reason ring-based metro architectures have historically been popular.

Modern networks can use more complex topologies, including rings, meshes, and provider-specific architectures.

# 17. Redundancy

MAN infrastructure mein redundancy very important hai.

Suppose:

```text
Office A -------- Office B
```

Single link fail:

```text
Office A ----X---- Office B
```

Connectivity lost.

Better:

```text
             Link 1
Office A ------------- Office B
    \                    /
     \                  /
      +---- Link 2 ----+
```

Now one path fail hone par alternate path available ho sakta hai.

# 20. Layer 2 MAN vs Layer 3 MAN

Ye advanced but important distinction hai.

MAN service Layer 2 ya Layer 3 nature ki ho sakti hai.

## Layer 2 connectivity

Provider customer sites ko Ethernet-like connectivity provide kar sakta hai.

```text
Site A
 VLAN/Ethernet
    |
Provider L2 Service
    |
Site B
 VLAN/Ethernet
```

Customer routing khud manage kar sakta hai.

---

## Layer 3 connectivity

Provider routing service provide kar sakta hai.

```text
Site A
   |
Customer Router
   |
Provider L3 Network
   |
Customer Router
   |
Site B
```

Provider routing infrastructure traffic ko appropriate destination ki taraf forward karta hai.

# 21. MAN and MPLS

**MPLS = Multiprotocol Label Switching**

MPLS historically enterprise WAN/MAN/provider networks mein extensively use hua hai.

Conceptually:

```text
Customer Site A
      |
    CE Router
      |
    PE Router
      |
   MPLS Core
      |
    PE Router
      |
    CE Router
      |
Customer Site B
```

Terms:

* CE = Customer Edge
* PE = Provider Edge

Provider network:

```text
PE ─── P ─── P ─── PE
```

MPLS labels traffic forwarding mein use hote hain.

Important:

> MPLS khud "MAN" nahi hai. MPLS ek forwarding technology hai jo provider networks mein MAN/WAN services implement karne ke liye use ho sakti hai.

```text

```

### Step 5 — Ping local gateway


```bash

```

### Step 7 — Trace path

```bash
tracepath 10.20.1.10
```


# 35. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: "MAN is just a large LAN."

Not exactly.

MAN is a **metropolitan network scope**, often interconnecting multiple LANs/sites.

```text
LAN → local site
MAN → multiple local sites across metro area
```

---

## Mistake 2: "MAN is a protocol."

Wrong.

MAN is a network classification.

Technologies can include:

* Metro Ethernet
* Fiber
* MPLS
* Optical transport
* Wireless links

---

## Mistake 3: "MPLS = MAN."

Wrong.

MPLS is a networking technology.

It can be used in MAN/WAN provider networks.

```text
MAN ≠ MPLS
WAN ≠ MPLS
```

---

## Mistake 4: "Fiber = MAN."

Wrong.

Fiber is a physical medium.

It can be used in:

* LAN
* MAN
* WAN
* Data centers
* Long-haul networks

---

## Mistake 5: "MAN always belongs to one company."

Not necessarily.

A MAN can be:

* Enterprise-owned
* Telecom-provider operated
* Carrier-managed
* Public/private infrastructure

# 36. Interview Questions

### Q1. What is MAN?

**Answer:**

MAN stands for Metropolitan Area Network. It is a network designed to connect multiple LANs or sites across a metropolitan or city-scale geographic area.

---

### Q2. Difference between LAN and MAN?

**Answer:**

LAN covers a local site such as a home, office or building. MAN covers a larger metropolitan area and can interconnect multiple LANs/sites.

---

### Q3. Difference between MAN and WAN?

**Answer:**

MAN generally operates at metropolitan/city scale, while WAN covers much larger geographic areas such as multiple cities, countries or continents.

---

### Q4. Is MAN a protocol?

**Answer:**

No. MAN is a network classification based on geographic scope.

---

### Q5. What technologies are commonly used in MAN?

**Answer:**

Metro Ethernet, carrier Ethernet, fiber-optic networks, MPLS-based services, optical transport technologies and some wireless metropolitan links.

---

### Q6. What is Metro Ethernet?

**Answer:**

Metro Ethernet is Ethernet-based connectivity delivered across a metropolitan area, often by a service provider.

---

### Q7. Why is fiber commonly used in MAN?

**Answer:**

Because fiber provides high bandwidth, long-distance transmission, low attenuation and immunity to electromagnetic interference.

---

### Q8. Why is redundancy important in MAN?

**Answer:**

Because MAN can connect critical sites such as offices and data centers. Redundant paths reduce the impact of a single link or device failure.

---

### Q9. What is MPLS?

**Answer:**

MPLS is a packet-forwarding technology that uses labels to influence forwarding. It is widely used in provider and enterprise WAN/metro services.

---

### Q10. Is MPLS the same as MAN?

**Answer:**

No. MAN describes network scope, while MPLS is a forwarding technology that can be used to implement services across MAN/WAN networks.

---

### Q11. How would you troubleshoot a MAN connectivity issue from Linux?

**Answer:**

Start with:

```bash
ip link
ip addr
ip route
ip neigh
ping <destination>
tracepath <destination>
ss -tulnp
sudo tcpdump -i eth0
```

Then check switch/router/VLAN/firewall/provider link status.

---

### Q12. What is the role of a router in MAN?

**Answer:**

Routers provide Layer-3 connectivity between different sites/subnets and forward packets based on routing information.

---

### Q13. What is the role of a switch?

**Answer:**

A switch primarily provides Layer-2 connectivity and forwards Ethernet frames based on MAC addresses.

---

### Q14. Why is MAN important for data centers?

**Answer:**

It can provide metropolitan connectivity between offices, primary data centers, disaster recovery sites, backup locations and other critical infrastructure.

```text
34. INTERVIEW Q&A

Q1. What is MAN?
A:
MAN stands for Metropolitan Area Network. It connects multiple LANs/sites across a metropolitan or city-scale geographic area.

Q2. Difference between LAN and MAN?
A:
LAN covers a local site such as an office or building. MAN connects multiple LANs/sites across a metropolitan area.

Q3. Difference between MAN and WAN?
A:
MAN operates at metropolitan/city scale, while WAN covers much larger geographic areas such as multiple cities, countries or continents.

Q4. Is MAN a protocol?
A:
No. MAN is a network scope/classification.

Q5. What technologies are used in MAN?
A:
Metro Ethernet, carrier Ethernet, fiber-optic networks, MPLS-based services, optical transport and some wireless metropolitan technologies.

Q6. What is Metro Ethernet?
A:
Ethernet-based connectivity delivered across a metropolitan area, commonly by a service provider.

Q7. Why is fiber common in MAN?
A:
Fiber provides high bandwidth, long-distance transmission, low attenuation and immunity to electromagnetic interference.

Q8. Why is redundancy important in MAN?
A:
MAN often connects critical sites such as offices and data centers. Redundant paths improve availability and reduce downtime.

Q9. What is MPLS?
A:
MPLS is a packet-forwarding technology that uses labels and is commonly used in provider and enterprise WAN/metro networks.

Q10. Is MPLS the same as MAN?
A:
No. MAN defines geographic scope; MPLS is a networking/forwarding technology.

Q11. What is the role of router in MAN?
A:
Router provides Layer-3 connectivity between different networks/sites and forwards packets based on routing information.

Q12. How would you troubleshoot MAN connectivity from Linux?
A:
Use:

    ip link
    ip addr
    ip route
    ip neigh
    ping
    tracepath
    ss
    tcpdump

Then inspect switches, routers, VLANs, firewalls and provider links.
```
